## 4. Portfolio Sitemap

### Initial Sitemap

The initial portfolio sitemap was intentionally kept small:

**Home → Work → About → Contact**

The purpose was to create only the pages necessary to move a visitor from understanding my claim to believing it and then taking the primary action.

### Page Roles

| Page | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Communicate the ML proof statement and provide immediate evidence. |
| **Work** | Present selected ML projects and connect them to detailed case studies. |
| **About** | Establish who I am through relevant education, experience and background. |
| **Contact** | Give the visitor one clear action: contact me about an ML/AI internship opportunity. |

### Case Study Structure

Case Studies are not treated as separate top-level navigation items. They are accessed through the Work page.

Each project case study will follow a structure such as:

1. Problem
2. Dataset
3. Approach
4. Model
5. Evaluation
6. Results
7. Limitations
8. Key Insights
9. Next Steps

Technical skills and tools will be demonstrated within the context of the projects rather than presented only as a standalone list.

---

## 4A. Portfolio Sitemap Pressure Test

I used my configured Claude Project to pressure-test the portfolio sitemap against my proof statement, target audience and primary action.

### Pressure-Test Prompt

I asked Claude to critically evaluate:

- Whether every page earns its place
- Whether the sitemap proves my ML claim
- Whether the structure leads toward the primary action
- Whether anything is unnecessary
- Whether anything important is missing
- What changes should be made

The complete pressure-test prompt and Claude's response are included in `PressureTest.png`.

### Key Findings From Claude

Claude identified the following important points:

1. **Work and Contact clearly earn their place.**
2. **Home needs to show evidence**, not only make a general ML claim.
3. A standalone **Skills section should be avoided** because the portfolio is intended to prove ability through practical work.
4. **Case Studies should exist under Work** rather than being treated as a separate top-level navigation item.
5. Each case study should include a dedicated **Limitations** section.
6. The Work section should prioritize strong projects backed by real data, evaluation and code rather than increasing the number of projects for appearance.

### Most Important Feedback

The most important recommendation from the pressure test was:

> Put proof on the Home page.

This means the Home page should not only state my ML claim. It should immediately show evidence through my strongest projects.

---

## 4B. Revision After Pressure Test

Based on Claude's feedback, I will make the following changes to the portfolio:

### 1. Add a Proof Strip to Home

I will add a proof strip featuring my strongest ML projects with concrete evidence and links to their case studies.

This will allow an ML/AI hiring manager to see evidence of my capabilities quickly instead of having to trust a general claim first.

### 2. Remove the Standalone Skills Section

I will not rely on a separate list of technologies as the primary evidence of my ability.

Instead, technologies will be demonstrated inside project case studies where they are connected to actual work.

For example:

- Python → preprocessing/model development
- Pandas → data analysis and preprocessing
- Scikit-learn → model training and evaluation
- Flask or other deployment tools → turning models into usable applications

### 3. Add Limitations to Case Studies

Each case study will explicitly describe model or solution limitations.

This will help demonstrate that I understand not only the results of an ML solution but also where it may fail and how it could be improved.

### 4. Keep Case Studies Under Work

Case Studies will be accessed through the Work page rather than being presented as a separate top-level navigation item.

### Final Sitemap

The revised structure is:

**Home → Work → About → Contact**

with:

**Work → Individual Case Studies**

and the Home page containing a short **Proof Strip** linking to the strongest case studies.

### Main Revision

The single most important change I will make is to add project evidence directly to the Home page so that an ML/AI hiring manager can see proof of my claim immediately.

## 6. Key Learnings

This audit helped me understand that AI should not be used in the same way for every task.

For DSA and Machine Learning, AI is most useful as a collaborator. It can help me understand problems, identify mistakes, explore alternatives and improve my reasoning without replacing the learning process.

For repetitive activities such as documentation and initial content drafting, AI can save time by creating a first version that I review and personalize.

I also identified activities where AI should not replace me. College examinations, attending classes and making important personal or career decisions require my own responsibility and judgment.

The portfolio sitemap exercise also showed me that a strong portfolio should not simply list skills. It should provide evidence that supports the central claim and guide the visitor toward one clear action.

The three workflows I selected — DSA problem solving, Machine Learning project development and internship assignment completion — will allow me to evaluate how AI can improve my productivity while maintaining my own understanding and decision-making.
